1. Macroscopic Quantum State BCS Theory

In superconductors, electrons with opposite momentum and spin bind into Cooper pairs of charge $q = -2e$ and mass $2m_e$.

Ψ(r, t) = √ns ei θ(r, t)

Because all $10^{22}$ Cooper pairs share the identical quantum state, the phase $\theta$ is a macroscopic observable. Across an insulating barrier (SIS junction), weak quantum mechanical tunneling couples the two wavefunctions:

Is = Ic sin(φ)
dφ/dt = (2e / ℏ) V = (2π / Φ0) V

DC Josephson Effect: A zero-voltage supercurrent flows up to $I_c$.
AC Josephson Effect: A constant voltage $V$ produces GHz oscillations at $f_J = 483.6 \text{ MHz}/\mu\text{V}$.

2. SQUID Double-Slit Analogy Fluxoid Quantization

Single-valuedness of the macroscopic wavefunction around any closed superconducting loop requires:

∮ ∇θ · dl = 2π m &implies; Φtotal = m Φ0

For two junctions in parallel, the total current is the quantum superposition:

I(φ0) = Ic1 sin(φ0 - πΦ/Φ0) + Ic2 sin(φ0 + πΦ/Φ0)

When symmetric ($I_{c1} = I_{c2} = I_c$):

Ic,max(Φ) = 2 Ic |cos(π Φ / Φ0)|

This is the exact optical equivalent of Young's double-slit experiment performed with matter waves of condensed Cooper pairs!

3. Nanoelectronic Applications Qubits & Sensors

1. Superconducting Qubits (Transmons):
The Josephson junction is the only known non-dissipative, non-linear inductor:

LJ(φ) = Φ0 / (2π Ic cos φ)

This non-linearity breaks the harmonic oscillator degeneracy, creating an artificial two-level quantum atom (the building block of Google and IBM quantum computers).

2. Ultra-Sensitive Magnetometers:
By converting magnetic flux into measurable voltage oscillations ($\Delta V \approx 100\,\mu\text{V}/\Phi_0$), SQUIDs detect fields down to $10^{-15}\text{ T}$ (1 fT)—capable of recording synaptic neuronal firing in human brains (MEG).
